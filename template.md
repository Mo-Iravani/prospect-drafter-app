The research paragraph — the part that matters:

- **Named specifics are what make this email work.** "Given your strong footprint in projects
  like Isimi Lagos" is the model. A named development, service line, office location or
  market position, taken from their own website.
- **If the research is thin, degrade gracefully.** Drop the "Given [proof point]" clause and
  keep the sentence general: "...there is a natural alignment between your growth objectives
  and WLCC's global network." A shorter honest sentence beats an invented project name.
- **If there is no usable research at all**, write: "Following your recent interaction with
  the World Luxury Chamber of Commerce, I wanted to introduce our work more fully." Then go
  straight to the closing paragraph. Never invent a project, a location, or a specialism.
- **Never guess from the company name.** A firm called "Luxury Estates Ltd" tells you nothing
  about what they actually build or where.

Other rules for this stage:

- Under 150 words in the body.
- Greeting is "Dear [first name],". Sign-off is "Best regards, Tooka" for this first email.
  (The follow-up uses "Kind regards" — that difference is intentional, keep it.)
- International business English. Clear and formal, never florid.
- Match the seniority in Job Title where it is natural to do so: a CFO wants the commercial
  case, a marketing director wants the mechanism, a founder wants the opportunity.
- Never overstate what WLCC offers. Stick to what the sender description says.
- Subject line: short, specific to them, no clickbait and no exclamation marks.
