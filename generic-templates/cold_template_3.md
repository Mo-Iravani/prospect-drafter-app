COLD CALL — THIRD EMAIL. The last one. Two emails have gone unanswered.

The job here is to close the loop gracefully and leave the door open. It is not a third pitch:
email two already made the offer and already said "keep us in mind". Saying it a third time at
the same length reads as pressure, and on a cold list that is how a company gets remembered
badly and reported as spam.

---

SUGGESTED COPY — keep the voice, keep it this short:

Dear {{first_name}},

I have written a couple of times about the World Luxury Chamber of Commerce and do not want to
crowd your inbox, so this will be my last note on it.

[OPTIONAL SINGLE SENTENCE — only if there is genuinely useful research on [COMPANY], and only
if it can be said without pitching. Something that shows you understood their business and
closes warmly. If there is nothing real to say, omit this entirely.]

If WLCC membership becomes relevant for [COMPANY] later, my door is open and I would be glad to
hear from you. Otherwise I will leave you to it, with best wishes for the year ahead.

Kind regards,
Tooka

---

Rules for this stage:

- Five sentences at most. This must be visibly shorter than email two.
- No new pitch and no restating what WLCC offers for a third time. They have read it twice.
- Make "no" completely costless. Do not ask them to reply in order to opt out, and do not ask
  them to forward it to a colleague.
- No deadlines, no "last chance to join", no scarcity, no discount. Manufactured urgency in a
  third unanswered cold email is the fastest way to be remembered badly.
- Tone: warm, unhurried, slightly personal. You are ending this well, not extracting a result.
- Do not imply any relationship exists. Two unanswered emails is not a relationship.
- Keep "Dear [first name]," and the "Kind regards, Tooka" sign-off.
- Subject line: quiet and human. Never "Final reminder" or anything with an exclamation mark.
