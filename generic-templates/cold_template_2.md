COLD CALL — SECOND EMAIL. Sent about a week after the first, to people who did not reply.

Written to sit behind the cold first email: they now know who WLCC is, because email one said
so. This email does not re-introduce the chamber from scratch. Its job is to give one new
reason to engage and to make replying cheap.

Built from Tooka's approved follow-up copy, adjusted for a cold list — a cold prospect has no
"previous interaction" to refer back to, only the previous email.

APPROVED COPY — follow this closely:

Dear {{first_name}},

I hope you are having a great week.

I wrote to you last week about the World Luxury Chamber of Commerce and wanted to check
whether this is something worth exploring at [COMPANY] at this stage.

[INSERT ONE SENTENCE HERE — the only genuinely new content in the email, and the reason it is

or a service line. It should read as a natural reason WLCC is relevant to them specifically.

If there is no usable research on this company, OMIT this sentence entirely and go straight to
the next paragraph. Do not invent anything. Do not substitute flattery.]

WLCC provides high-level visibility, cross-border connections, and targeted market access for

If exploring this is timely for you, let me know if you would be open to a quick 10-minute

Rules for this stage:

- Shorter than email one. Under 110 words in the body.
- The WLCC paragraph and the closing paragraph are approved copy. Reproduce them faithfully.
  You may adjust a few words so the inserted sentence flows — nothing more.
- You may tailor the WLCC paragraph slightly to the prospect's sector where it is honestly
  applicable (referring to real estate developers or advisory firms rather than
  "organizations"). Never overstate what WLCC offers.
- **Do not repeat email one's specific observation.** If the research only supports one
  observation and email one already used it, omit the personalised sentence rather than
  saying the same thing twice.
- Never write "just following up", "just bumping this", "did you get my last email" or
  "circling back". "I wrote to you last week" already covers it.
- Do not apologise for emailing again, and do not guilt them. Assume they are busy, not
  uninterested.
- Never claim they showed interest, opened the email, or were referred.
- Keep the greeting as "Dear [first name]," — not "Hi".
- International business English. Clear and formal, never stiff or florid.
- The sign-off stays "Kind regards, Tooka".
- Subject line: short, specific to them where possible, no clickbait and no "re:" prefix.
