COLD CALL — FIRST EMAIL. Tooka's approved copy.

Source: "First email sample - cold call.docx". The structure below is the approved one. Keep the
voice, keep the paragraph order, keep the WLCC framing. Two slots change per prospect: the
observation in paragraph two and the sector phrase in paragraph three.

This is cold outreach. The person has had NO prior interaction with WLCC. Never write
"following your recent interaction with WLCC", and never imply they enquired, attended a
webinar, or were referred. They did not.

---

APPROVED COPY — follow this closely:

Dear {{first_name}},

I hope this email finds you well.

I've been following [COMPANY] and I am particularly impressed by [ONE SPECIFIC, VERIFIABLE
ASPECT OF THEIR BUSINESS, taken from the research. In the approved sample this was "your
comprehensive approach to wealth management and fiduciary services". It must be something they
actually say about themselves: a named service line, a stated specialism, a market position, a
named development. Not their size, not a compliment about their website.]

I'm currently reaching out to a select number of firms within the [THEIR SECTOR — a short,
accurate phrase for the space they operate in, drawn from the research rather than copied from
the Industry column. The approved sample used "private wealth and independent advisory" for a
firm the sheet lists only as "Banking". Other examples: "luxury residential development",
"international fund administration", "family office and fiduciary"] sectors that I believe
could be a strong fit for the World Luxury Chamber of Commerce (WLCC), particularly from an
international expansion, cross-industry network, and premium positioning perspective.

Would you be open to a short introductory conversation to explore whether this could be
relevant for your firm?

Looking forward to hearing from you.

Best Regards,
Tooka

---

The two variable slots — the part that matters:

- **Paragraph two is the whole email.** "I am particularly impressed by your comprehensive
  approach to wealth management and fiduciary services" is the model. It is admiration pinned
  to a specific, checkable fact. That is the line that makes this read as written for them.
- **The "Why WLCC?" note in the sheet is gold.** If the sheet supplies one, it is a human
  judgement about why this company fits, and it should steer both slots. Trust it over anything
  you infer from the website. Never quote it verbatim — it is internal shorthand
  ("award winning wealth management firm, legacy, active" becomes proper prose).
- **The sector phrase must be honest.** If the research does not tell you what space they work
  in, write a wider but true phrase rather than a precise guess. "the private wealth sector"
  beats inventing "the Monaco yacht brokerage sector".
- **If the research is thin**, replace the observation with something true and general drawn
  from what they do say about themselves, and keep the email short. A shorter honest email
  beats an invented specialism.
- **If there is no usable research at all**, drop paragraph two entirely, open the third
  paragraph with "I'm currently reaching out to a select number of firms that I believe could
  be a strong fit for the World Luxury Chamber of Commerce (WLCC)...", and say so in
  personalisation_note. Never invent a project, a location, or a specialism.
- **Never guess from the company name.** A firm called "Luxury Estates Ltd" tells you nothing
  about what they actually build or where.

Other rules for this stage:

- Under 120 words in the body. The approved sample is 98. This email works because it is short.
- Six short paragraphs, one sentence each, in the order above. Do not merge them and do not add
  a seventh.
- Greeting is "Dear [first name],". Sign-off is "Best Regards, Tooka" for this first email.
  (Emails two and three use "Kind regards" — that difference is intentional, keep it.)
- The third paragraph's WLCC framing is approved copy: "a select number of firms", "a strong
  fit", "international expansion, cross-industry network, and premium positioning". Reproduce
  those phrases. Only the sector slot changes.
- The approved copy contains NO reference to a phone call. If, and only if, the prompt
  explicitly says a call was attempted, you may add one short sentence after the greeting:
  "I tried calling earlier to introduce myself but wasn't able to reach you." Nothing more.
  If the prompt does not say a call was attempted, write no such sentence — it would be untrue.
- International business English. Clear and formal, never florid.
- Match the seniority in Job Title where it is natural to do so: a CFO wants the commercial
  case, a Head of Communications wants the positioning and the network, a founder wants the
  opportunity.
- Never overstate what WLCC offers. Do not name a membership tier, quote a price, or promise a
  specific introduction.
- Subject line: short, specific to them, no clickbait and no exclamation marks.
